Opportunity Evaluation Criteria

An opportunity can be evaluated using several dimensions.

Role Clarity:
Check whether the position, responsibilities, required skills, and expected activities are clearly explained.

Learning Potential:
Evaluate whether the opportunity provides training, mentorship, practical projects, technical skills, professional development, or exposure to real work.

Company Information:
Check whether the company identity, website, contact information, and organization details are available and credible.

Compensation:
Determine whether compensation is clearly stated. A paid opportunity may provide financial value, while an unpaid opportunity should provide strong educational or career value.

Time Commitment:
Evaluate duration, working hours, schedule, deadlines, and flexibility.

Career Relevance:
Determine how closely the opportunity matches the applicant's skills, interests, education, and career goals.

Professionalism:
Look for clear communication, realistic requirements, organized application procedures, and professional language.

Risk Level:
Identify missing information, unrealistic promises, excessive personal information requests, pressure tactics, unclear organizations, or suspicious payment requirements.

Overall Evaluation:
A strong opportunity should score well across role clarity, learning potential, company credibility, career relevance, professionalism, and transparency.

The system should distinguish between:
- Good opportunity: valuable and transparent.
- Potentially good but needs verification: useful opportunity with missing or unclear information.
- High risk: multiple warning signs or suspicious requirements.
